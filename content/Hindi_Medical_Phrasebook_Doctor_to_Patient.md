# NOTES FOR TAMIL/ENGLISH SPEAKING DOCTORS

## Pronunciation Tips for Hindi Medical Terms

1. **Retroflex sounds**: Hindi has distinctive "ṭ" and "ḍ" sounds (like ट and ड). Practice these — they sound different from Tamil retroflex sounds.

2. **Aspirated consonants**: Add a puff of air after "bh", "dh", "gh" — e.g., बुखार (bukhār), घाव (ghāv).

3. **The "v" and "w" sounds**: In Hindi, these are often interchangeable. Many speakers use "v" for both.

4. **Nasal sounds**: Master the "ṅ" sound — e.g., इंजेक्शन (injection).

5. **The "ī" and "ee"**: Both represent the same long "i" sound — e.g., दवाई (davāī) = medicine.

6. **Polite forms**: Always use "आप" (aap - formal 'you') with patients. Never use "तू" (too - very informal).

7. **Adding "-ji" or "-sahab"**: Add respect — "डॉक्टर साहब" (Doctor Sahab), "दीदी" (Didi - for older female), "भैया" (Bhaiya - for older male).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Don't confuse "क्या" (kya = what/question marker) with "कब" (kab = when)
- "नहीं" (nahin = no/not) goes after the verb, not before
- "बहुत" (bahut = very/much) comes before adjectives: बहुत अच्छा (bahut achha = very good)
- Hindi uses postpositions, not prepositions: "पेट में" (pet mein = in the stomach), not "in pet"
